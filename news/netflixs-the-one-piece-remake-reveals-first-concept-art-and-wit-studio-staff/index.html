Netflix Releases First Concept Art and Staff Details for 'The One Piece' Remake

The highly anticipated reboot drops its first visual assets, confirming a heavyweight production team ready to modernize the East Blue saga without the filler.

By Christopher Ivanov
Apr 8, 2026

Netflix just dropped the first visual evidence that its highly anticipated anime remake, The One Piece, is actively moving through production. A newly released promotional video highlights fresh character designs and expansive concept art for the upcoming adaptation.

Produced by Wit Studio, this series will completely recreate Eiichiro Oda’s pirate saga, beginning with the foundational East Blue arc. Fans can expect a streamlined, filler-free experience that trades the traditional Sunday morning broadcast constraints for premium, late-night animation quality.

The project originally made headlines during its announcement at Jump Festa 2024. Now, the streaming platform is proving the concept is more than just a boardroom idea.

With over 1,100 episodes in the original Toei Animation run, jumping into the franchise is famously difficult for newcomers.

This fresh adaptation serves as a modern entry point, leveraging the latest digital production techniques to offer a familiar yet entirely updated take on Luffy’s early adventures.

What the New Concept Art Video Reveals

The latest promotional footage offers a direct look at the “image boards” guiding the visual direction.

These conceptual sheets are a critical first step in anime production.

They establish the precise color palette, lighting design, and overall environmental atmosphere long before actual key animation begins.

Wit Studio is clearly aiming for a cinematic aesthetic.

The video showcases early sketches of the core Straw Hat crew alongside iconic East Blue locations.

The artwork displays a sharper, more textured style compared to the flat, bright colors of the late 90s broadcast.

According to the official YouTube description, the studio plans to tap into “the expressive potential of modern technology.”

This points to the likely integration of dynamic 3D camera work and digital compositing.

Those specific digital tools were entirely unavailable when the original series premiered in 1999.

Meet the Heavyweight Team at Wit Studio

A production is only as strong as its staff.

Netflix has assembled an absolute powerhouse crew for this reboot.

The roster is packed with industry veterans who have shaped some of the biggest global hits of the last decade.

Key Staff Members Include:

  • Director: Masashi Koizuka (Attack on Titan, Moonrise)
  • Assistant Director: Hideaki Abe (Detective Conan: The Scarlet Bullet, Suicide Squad ISEKAI)
  • Character Designers / Chief Animation Directors: Kyoji Asano (Spy x Family, Attack on Titan) and Takatoshi Honda (The First Slam Dunk)
  • Series Scripts: Taku Kishimoto (Haikyu!!, Ranking of Kings)
  • Action Animators: Ken Imaizumi (Boruto) and Shuhei Fukuda (My Hero Academia)
  • Art Director: Tomonori Kuroda (A Certain Scientific Railgun)
  • Image Board / Creature Design: Yasuhiro Kajino (In/Spectre)
  • Prop Design: Eri Taguchi (Vampire in the Garden)

Revisiting the East Blue Saga: Why Start Over?

Eiichiro Oda’s original manga chapters covering the East Blue storyline were published in the late 1990s.

The narrative establishes the core crew, Zoro, Nami, Usopp, and Sanji, while setting the emotional stakes for the entire journey.

Yet, the original anime adaptation shows its age.

Standard definition aspect ratios and traditional cel animation techniques make the earliest episodes feel entirely disconnected from the visual spectacle of modern arcs like Wano.

A remake bridges this visual gap.

Newcomers can experience the emotional weight of Nami’s backstory in Arlong Park without needing to push through dozens of poorly paced, visibly dated episodes.

Wit Studio has a clear mandate to make these foundational stories look as spectacular as the modern product.

Eliminating the Filler Episodes

For decades, the original anime suffered from severe pacing issues.

Because it aired almost every single week, the show constantly risked overtaking the manga’s publication.

To prevent a collision, directors often stretched a single chapter across a full 20-minute episode using prolonged reaction shots and extended flashbacks.

Wit Studio President George Wada confirmed this remake will abandon that outdated model.

In a recent interview, Wada explicitly stated the new series will feature tighter pacing without any unnecessary padding.

The goal is to produce a show with the same quality as their late-night hits like Vinland Saga and Spy x Family.

The Late-Night Visual Upgrade

Historically, the original series occupied a Sunday morning timeslot in Japan.

That placement restricted certain visual choices to remain suitable for young children.

Adopting a late-night production mentality for streaming gives Wit Studio the freedom to present fights and dramatic moments with intense clarity.

Wada emphasized that while the new team wants to push boundaries, they fully respect the foundational work Toei Animation has done over the last quarter-century.

The Economics of an Anime Reboot

Re-animating an ongoing series is highly unusual in the Japanese broadcast market.

Usually studios only reboot properties after a manga concludes, such as with Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood or Fruits Basket.

Undertaking this project while Toei Animation continues to produce the original series is a radical industry experiment.

The strategy makes perfect financial sense for a global tech platform.

Netflix lacks ownership over the legacy broadcast rights in many international territories.

Producing a fresh adaptation guarantees the streaming service exclusive, permanent distribution rights to the IP.

Furthermore a highly polished 25-episode season is infinitely more marketable to the casual algorithm scroller than a playlist of a thousand legacy episodes.

The End of the Weekly Grind: Toei Animation’s Strategic Shift

While Wit builds the future, the legacy studio is drastically altering its own approach.

Toei Animation’s original One Piece series has finally abandoned its relentless year-round broadcast schedule.

As of 2026, the studio reduced its annual output to a maximum of 26 episodes.

This is a landmark shift for the industry.

After wrapping up the Egghead arc last year, the production committee instituted a three-month hiatus from January through March 2026.

The series just returned on April 5 for the start of the highly anticipated Elbaph arc.

Plus, the broadcaster moved the show to a late-night 11:15 p.m. timeslot.

According to Toei, this new seasonal schedule allows the staff to incorporate better tempo while matching the manga’s density.

Moving away from the weekly grind ensures animators have the time necessary to deliver feature-film quality visuals on a television budget.

During the recent break, the network kept fans engaged by airing One Piece Log: Fish-Man Island Saga, a condensed 21-episode version of the classic arc featuring reshot scenes and retouched art.

Netflix’s Expanding Grand Line Fleet

The Wit Studio remake is just one component of a much broader corporate strategy.

Netflix is actively maneuvering to become the exclusive global hub for Eiichiro Oda’s creation.

Funding a brand-new animated entry point allows the platform to capture a younger demographic.

The live-action adaptation continues to perform exceptionally well for the streamer.

Season 2 debuted on March 10, 2026, utilizing a unique theatrical strategy that played the first two episodes in over 200 theaters across the United States, Canada, and Japan.

The company has already confirmed that Season 3 will arrive in 2027.

Looking ahead, the franchise footprint will only grow.

An animated Lego special is slated to stream on September 29, highlighting how flexible the property has become.

Between the live-action blockbuster, the Toei continuation, and the Wit Studio reboot, the pirate saga is currently experiencing unprecedented mainstream saturation.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

When is The One Piece anime remake releasing on Netflix?

An official release date has not been announced. However, considering the recent release of character designs and the finalized staff roster, an expected premiere window of late 2026 or early 2027 is highly probable.

Is the original One Piece anime ending because of the remake?

No. Toei Animation is continuing the original series. The original anime recently shifted to a seasonal model, capping at 26 episodes a year to maintain high animation quality. The series just began the Elbaph arc in April 2026.

Will the Netflix remake have filler episodes?

Wit Studio President George Wada confirmed that the remake will feature tight pacing. The production will eliminate the unnecessary padding that was required in the original weekly television broadcast.

What arcs will the new anime cover?

The initial announcement confirmed the series will start by adapting the East Blue Saga. While the phrasing hints that subsequent arcs like Alabasta or Skypiea could be adapted later, Netflix has only officially committed to the East Blue portion so far.

Who is animating the One Piece remake?

The new series is being animated by Wit Studio. This is the celebrated production house behind the first three seasons of Attack on Titan, Vinland Saga, and Spy x Family.
