Wobble Wobble: The Bird-Based Bomb Disposal Sim Hitting Steam This April

Indie developer Team Baeb Sae is bringing high-tension bomb disposal to your PC. Discover why this Jenga-style physics puzzler is the ultimate test of your mouse-control skills.

By Justin Ali
Mar 31, 2026

Indie developer Team Baeb Sae is officially launching Wobble Wobble, a high-tension, physics-based bomb disposal puzzle game, on Steam on April 9, 2026. Players take on the role of a rookie sparrow agent tasked with defusing explosives hidden in fragile, Jenga-like structures using precise, mouse-only controls.

If you are a fan of games that test your steady hand and puzzle-solving logic under pressure, this avian-themed thriller is about to become your newest obsession.

Wobble Wobble mixes the nerve-wracking tension of real-world bomb defusal with the unpredictable chaos of a simulated physics engine.

Forget traditional shooters or sprawling RPGs. The biggest gaming trend right now is hyper-focused, physics-driven micro-experiences.

So let’s explore everything you need to know about this upcoming indie darling, from its unique gameplay mechanics to expert tips on how to survive your first day on the job.

What is Wobble Wobble?

At its core, Wobble Wobble is a physics simulation game built around a simple but terrifying concept: avian bomb terrorism.

You are a bird. You defuse bombs. It sounds absurd, but the execution is purely strategic.

You play as a rookie bomb disposal agent thrust into the field after your veteran mentor is unexpectedly taken out of action.

The world is suddenly under siege by the nefarious “Villainous Sparrows.” As the last sparrow standing, the fate of the globe rests on your tiny, trembling wings.

The game requires you to locate and remove bombs hidden inside complex, unstable structures.

You must push, pull, and toss surrounding blocks to get to the explosives without letting the entire structure collapse.

One wrong twitch of the mouse, and the whole stage goes boom.

The “Jenga” Effect in Gaming

We’ve all played Jenga. You know that heart-dropping moment when you pull a block and the tower leans a fraction of an inch? Wobble Wobble digitizes that exact feeling.

Unlike traditional puzzle games where solutions are static, physics simulators are dynamic.

The weight distribution changes with every block you move.

You aren’t just looking for the right path; you are constantly fighting gravity, friction, and your own nervous energy.

Gameplay Mechanics (Precision is Everything)

Many modern games rely on complex controller inputs, massive combo chains, or lightning-fast reflexes.

Wobble Wobble goes in the complete opposite direction by utilizing a mouse-only control scheme.

This design choice strips away the barrier to entry, making the game accessible to anyone.

But simple controls do not mean an easy game.

In fact, relying solely on your mouse pointer increases the tension significantly.

Push, Pull, and Toss

Your interaction with the game world is limited to three basic physical actions:

  • Pushing: Gently nudging a block out of the way to test the structural integrity of the pile.
  • Pulling: Carefully sliding a piece toward you, hoping the blocks resting on top of it settle safely into place.
  • Tossing: Physically grabbing and throwing non-explosive debris out of the way to clear your workspace.

The Realistic Physics Engine

The true star of the show here is the realistic physics engine.

According to the developers at Team Baeb Sae, the game is designed to react to even the slightest vibration.

When you pull a block out from the bottom of a stack, the structure doesn’t just fall; it sways, settles, and groans under the shifted weight.

Understanding center of mass and momentum is the only way you will survive the later stages of the game.

Incredible Stage Design

One of the most fascinating aspects of Wobble Wobble is its sense of scale.

You are playing as a small bird, which means your perspective on the world is entirely skewed.

The development team has leaned into this concept by creating a massive variety of bomb sites.

You aren’t just defusing bombs in military bases; you are saving everyday objects and global monuments from total destruction.

Micro vs. Macro Levels

  • Everyday Objects: Some stages take place in highly relatable, micro environments. You might find yourself carefully pulling keycaps off a mechanical keyboard on an office desk to reach a tiny explosive hidden underneath.
  • Global Landmarks: Other stages zoom out, placing you on massive structures like the Statue of Liberty. Here the stakes feel exponentially higher, as the wind and the sheer height of the structure add new layers of physics-based complexity to your mission.

This shifting sense of scale keeps the gameplay fresh.

Defusing a bomb on a desk requires a completely different mindset than defusing one clinging to the torch of Lady Liberty.

So why Physics Puzzlers Are Dominating Steam?

If you look at the Steam charts over the last few years, you’ll notice a distinct rise in the popularity of physics-based puzzle games.

But why are games like Wobble Wobble striking such a chord with gamers?

1. The Streamer Effect

First and foremost, these games are incredibly fun to watch.

When a player makes a tiny mistake and a massive tower of blocks comes crashing down, it creates an instant, highly shareable moment.

Twitch streamers and YouTube creators thrive on games that generate genuine reactions of panic, relief, and hilarious failure.

2. Emergent Gameplay

Physics games rely on “emergent gameplay.” This means that the developer doesn’t script every single event.

Instead they build a set of rules (gravity, friction, weight) and let the player loose.

This guarantees that no two playthroughs are ever exactly the same.

You might beat a level by perfectly sliding out a support beam, while your friend might beat the same level by “violently” throwing a block and getting lucky when the bomb bounces into a safe zone.

Tips for Surviving Your First Day

Stepping into the boots of a rookie bomb disposal agent isn’t easy.

If you want to defeat the Villainous Sparrows and keep your stress levels manageable, keep these expert strategies in mind when you boot up the game on April 9th.

  • Analyze Before You Act: Don’t just start clicking. Take a full 60 seconds at the start of every level to rotate the camera. Look for load-bearing blocks and identify the center of gravity.
  • Start from the Top: Whenever possible, clear debris from the top of the structure down. Removing weight from the upper levels makes the base significantly more stable for when you need to extract the actual bomb.
  • Lower Your Mouse DPI: If your gaming mouse has an adjustable DPI (Dots Per Inch) button, turn it down. Lower sensitivity means smoother, more deliberate cursor movements, which is crucial when one twitch can collapse the tower.
  • Use the “Nudge” Method: Never pull a block out in one fast motion. Nudge it a few millimeters and watch how the structure above it reacts. If the tower starts to sway heavily, stop and find another angle.

System Requirements (Can Your PC Handle the Physics?)

While physics simulation games are notorious for melting CPUs with complex collision calculations, Team Baeb Sae has optimized Wobble Wobble beautifully.

You won’t need a massive, glowing gaming rig to keep your sparrow agent in the field.

Because the game focuses on tightly controlled, closed-room puzzle environments rather than massive open worlds, the hardware barrier to entry is incredibly low.

Let’s break down exactly what you need under the hood to run this smoothly.

Minimum PC Requirements

If you are gaming on an older desktop or a standard work laptop, you are still in luck.

The minimum specs are highly forgiving.

  • OS: Windows 10 (64-bit)
  • Processor: Intel Core i3-6100 or AMD Ryzen 3 1200
  • Memory: 4 GB RAM
  • Graphics: Intel HD Graphics 630 or NVIDIA GT 1030
  • DirectX: Version 11
  • Storage: 1 GB available space

Recommended PC Requirements

For the absolute best experience, you will want to hit the recommended specs.

In a game where a single frame stutter or lag spike could cause your cursor to jerk and detonate a bomb, smooth performance is a matter of life and virtual death.

  • OS: Windows 10/11 (64-bit)
  • Processor: Intel Core i5-8400 or AMD Ryzen 5
  • Memory: 8 GB RAM
  • Graphics: NVIDIA GTX 1050 or AMD RX 560
  • DirectX: Version 12
  • Storage: 2 GB available space

Tip: Notice the processor jump to an Intel Core i5 or Ryzen 5 for the recommended settings. Real-time physics engines (calculating gravity, block friction, and momentum on the fly) lean heavily on your CPU rather than your graphics card. A GTX 1050 is an older, budget-friendly GPU, proving that processing power is the true bottleneck for realistic physics simulators.

Steam Deck Compatibility

With these official specs now in the wild, we can say that Wobble Wobble is practically custom-built for the Steam Deck.

The handheld’s custom APU effortlessly clears the recommended benchmark of a GTX 1050 and 8GB of RAM, meaning you can expect a rock-solid 60 frames per second on the go.

But the real advantage here is the hardware synergy.

Since the game utilizes a delicate, mouse-only control scheme, the Steam Deck’s haptic trackpads will shine.

Mapping your cursor to the right trackpad allows for microscopic, ultra-precise movements that a standard analog stick simply cannot replicate.

We fully expect this title to earn a green “Verified” checkmark shortly after its April 9th launch.

Games Like Wobble Wobble to Play While You Wait

April 9th is fast approaching, but if you need to scratch that physics-puzzle itch right now, there are a few excellent titles that share DNA with Wobble Wobble:

  • Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes: If it’s the bomb defusal tension you crave, this is the gold standard. It requires one player to defuse a bomb while others read the manual, creating pure, chaotic teamwork.
  • Surgeon Simulator: For those who love the “clumsy physics” aspect. Trying to perform delicate operations with intentionally difficult, physics-based controls will train your patience for Wobble Wobble.
  • Angry Birds: It might sound incredibly basic, but the core mechanics of analyzing structural weaknesses and predicting physics trajectories in Angry Birds are exactly what you’ll be doing here—just in reverse!
  • Teardown: If you want to understand how a voxel/block-based physics engine calculates structural integrity and collapse, Teardown is a brilliant sandbox to practice in.

The Verdict (A Must-Play Indie for Spring 2026)

Team Baeb Sae is tapping into a brilliant niche with this release. By combining the universal appeal of cute, avian protagonists with the nail-biting stress of high-stakes bomb disposal, they have crafted something truly unique.

Wobble Wobble strips away the bloated mechanics of AAA gaming and delivers a pure, focused challenge.

Mark your calendars for April 9, 2026, lower your mouse sensitivity, and get ready to save the world on trembling wings.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

When does Wobble Wobble release?

Wobble Wobble officially launches on PC via Steam on April 9, 2026. You can currently view the game and add it to your wishlist on the Steam Store.

Is Wobble Wobble a multiplayer game?

Based on current information from the developer, Wobble Wobble is a single-player physics puzzle experience where you act as the sole rookie bomb disposal agent.

What are the controls for Wobble Wobble?

The game features a completely mouse-only control scheme. Players use precise mouse movements and clicks to push, pull, and toss blocks without the need for complex keyboard inputs or controller combos.

What kind of levels are in the game?

The game features a wide variety of scales, ranging from micro environments like a desktop keyboard to massive, real-world landmarks like the Statue of Liberty.

Can I play Wobble Wobble with a controller?

While the game is optimized for “precise mouse control,” Steam’s controller configuration tools (especially on devices like the Steam Deck) usually allow players to map mouse movements to analog sticks or trackpads.
